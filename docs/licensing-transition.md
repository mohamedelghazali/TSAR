# TSAR Licensing Transition — Proposal Only

**Status: proposal; not an effective license.** The repository's current \`LICENSE\` file remains MIT. This document does not revoke, amend, or narrow any rights already granted under MIT.

## Proposed direction for a future release

The project owner is considering a custom source-available license tentatively named **TSAR Community License**.

The intended policy is:

- Permit personal, educational, and independent non-commercial research use.
- Permit non-commercial community contributions and redistribution under the same community terms.
- Require a separate prior written commercial license for commercial products, paid services, internal business deployment by for-profit organizations, hosted/SaaS or managed offerings, commercial consulting, and commissioned commercial development.
- Treat sponsored or funded research according to its actual purpose, control, deliverables, and commercial benefit rather than assuming every academic grant is commercial.
- Provide a separate commercial licensing path for organizations that need restricted, proprietary, or revenue-generating use.

The final license must define these terms precisely, including redistribution, sublicensing, derivative works, patents, notices, termination, warranty disclaimer, and limitation of liability.

## Required checks before changing LICENSE

1. Identify every copyright holder and contributor whose work is included.
2. Confirm the project owner has the rights or written permissions needed to relicense all relevant contributions.
3. Decide whether existing contributors may continue to contribute and under what contribution agreement.
4. Have an intellectual-property lawyer review the final text under Algerian law and the jurisdictions where TSAR will be distributed or used.
5. Publish the new license only for a clearly identified future version, with a clear statement that earlier MIT-licensed copies remain subject to their original MIT grants.

## Important distinction

A license that restricts commercial use is source-available, not OSI-compliant Open Source. The project must not advertise such a release as Open Source.

Until the above checks and legal review are complete, do not replace the current MIT \`LICENSE\` file or claim that commercial use is prohibited.
